> PLEA security audit, round 1, area A3: The market: PleaHook. PLEA is a sell-gated meme token on Uniswap v4 paired with IMD: anyone buys, selling needs a plea judged by a 30-seat IdentityMD oracle panel, approved pleas score season points for IMD and PLEA prizes, and every trade pays cashback in staked IMD through imd/acc's Stacker. Solidity 0.8.26, Foundry (cancun, via-IR), a Uniswap v4 hook, IdentityMD Intake v2 callbacks. ## Findings

### 1. Medium: A dust first buy consumes the PayoutsToPool exception; the next real buy's cashback, owner and prize shares are paid as ERC-6909 IMD claims instead of sIMD and IMD

`src/PleaHook.sol:561`

```
        if (buy) hasBought = true;
```

THREAT-MODEL invariant 19 allows two forms of cashback (sIMD through the Stacker, plain IMD if the credit reverts) and section 3 accepts only that the very first buy into an empty PoolManager routes its payouts to pool liquidity; README 'Fees' says 'There are no claims to settle later.' _payFees has a third, undocumented path: on any buy after the first where imd.balanceOf(poolManager) < cashback + owner + prize (1.5% of the fill), asClaims is true and, because firstBuy is already false, the cashback is minted to the trader as an ERC-6909 IMD claim (CashbackPaidClaims, no Stacker call, no sIMD), the owner's 0.75% is minted as a claim instead of being taken as IMD, and the prize share is minted to Seasons as a claim (Seasons redeems claims itself, so that share is not lost). hasBought is set by any buy, including one whose fee rounds to zero: a 1-wei exact-input buy (fee = 1 * 175 / 10175 = 0) right after seed leaves the PoolManager holding 1 wei of IMD and hasBought = true, so the first real buy of the launch pays claims. The trigger is permissionless and costs 1 wei plus gas (a front-run of the opening block), and the same condition recurs for any buy larger than about 66x the IMD the PoolManager holds, for example a buy after a post-death sell drains the market band's IMD (the PoolManager then holds only the hook's retained claims, 2.95 IMD in the probe, so a buy above about 200 IMD pays claims; a 100 IMD buy still paid sIMD). The trader needs a helper contract (unlock, burn, take) to turn the claim into IMD and gets no staking; the launch owner needs the same tooling. No value is lost, so Medium (griefing cheaper for the attacker than for the victim; accounting that departs from the agreed design). Merged from audit_economics (medium) and audit_permissions (low). Fix: treat any asClaims shortage the way the first buy is treated (route the unfunded shares to pool liquidity) or mint the cashback claim to the hook and redeem it to the Stacker for the trader at the next opportunity, so a trader never receives raw claims; at minimum set hasBought only when imdFee != 0 and document the claims path in THREAT-MODEL section 3.

### 2. Low: Exact-output swaps charge the specified-side fee on the requested amount, not on the fill: an exact-output IMD sell (every Letterbox fee sale) pays 1.72% instead of 1.75% and the pool share gets 0.22%

`src/PleaHook.sol:455`

```
        uint256 fee = exactIn ? amount * rate / (BPS + rate) : amount * rate / BPS;
```

Invariant 19: every trade pays exactly 2.0% on the filled amount, 0.25% of the IMD leg to pool liquidity. For exact input the hook takes rate/(BPS+rate) of the gross so the fee is rate/BPS of what the pool fills. For exact output it takes rate/BPS of the requested amount A; the pool then fills G = A + fee, and afterSwap's specDue = G * rate / BPS is larger than the reserved fee, so line 503 caps it back to the smaller value. The IMD fee on an exact-output sell is therefore 1.75% of A = 1.72% of G, while cashback, owner and prize are computed on imdBasis = specAmt = G, so the whole shortfall (0.03% of the fill) comes out of the pool-liquidity residual toPool = imdFee - out: 0.224% of the fill instead of 0.25% (0.47% instead of 0.50% once the Cabal is dead and the prize share joins it). While the Cabal lives this is the path of every Letterbox fee sale (CabalGate._swapFee is an exact-output IMD sell through the hook); after the death, any router's exact-output sell. Exact-output buys after the death show the same on the PLEA burn side (0.25% of the requested PLEA = 0.249% of the fill). Small, one-directional, systematic; the pool, not the trader, is short. Merged from audit_economics and audit_flow (both low). Fix: in beforeSwap reserve the exact-output fee as amount * rate / (BPS - rate) so that it equals rate/BPS of (amount + fee); the afterSwap cap then only trims rounding and partial fills.

**Reproduction**

Fixture: alice buys 1,000 IMD; skip(DEADMAN + 1) so a plain router may sell; a router calls PoolManager.swap with zeroForOne = pleaIsZero, amountSpecified = +100e18 (exact-output IMD), limit at the extreme, hookData = abi.encode(alice), settles PLEA and takes IMD. Measured on this commit (test/scratch/A3Judge.t.sol test_exactOutputSellFeeBase): alice receives exactly 100 IMD; the pool fills 101.75 IMD (fill = ownerShare * 10000 / 75); owner share 0.763125 IMD (0.75% of the fill, correct); cashback 0.50875 IMD (0.5% of the fill, correct); total IMD fee 1.75 IMD = 1.72% of the fill where 1.75% of the fill is 1.780625 IMD; pool share (pool + prize, Cabal dead) 0.478125 IMD where 0.5% of the fill is 0.50875 IMD: short 0.030625 IMD, i.e. the pool's own 0.25% lands at 0.22375.

### 3. Low: rebalance deploys the whole IMD reserve at a seller-chosen drained tick below the market band instead of reverting as the README states

`src/PleaHook.sol:837`

```
            int24 edge = spot < refTick ? spot : refTick;
```

README 'Wall placement guard' and invariant 21: a tick the pool sits at with no active liquidity is not a traded price; the guard ignores it 'so the wall is placed against the market once IMD is back', and 'until the next buy brings spot back from a drained tick, rebalance() reverts rather than placing a wall there.' _updateWallGuard does ignore the drained tick (it checks getLiquidity != 0), but _rebalance still takes the raw spot tick as a candidate for the band's near edge with no liquidity check, and only returns early when the aligned band collapses (a limit at the tick extreme). After the Cabal dies a seller can drain the market band's IMD with an exact-input sell whose price limit sits anywhere in the liquidity-free region below the band; a rebalance before the next buy (pendingRebalance is true as soon as 1 IMD of reserve or claims exists, which the drain's own 0.25% pool fee or one fundWall provides) deploys the entire reserve as a band whose near edge is the drained tick, far below the market band and the guard. After that rebalance retainedImd is dust and retainClaims is zero, so the reserve stays parked (6,000 ticks, about 45% in price, below the market in the probe) until WALL_THRESHOLD of new fees or a fundWall lets anyone rebalance again. No IMD leaves the pool and nobody buys the wall's IMD cheaply (a seller into it gets less IMD per PLEA than at the market), so this is a documented-behaviour mismatch with a bounded, self-repairing effect: Low, post-death only (while alive the gate uses the extreme limit and refuses partial fills). Merged from audit_economics and audit_flow (both low). Fix: in _rebalance skip the placement (return (work, acted)) when poolManager.getLiquidity(poolId()) == 0 at spot, the same test _updateWallGuard applies, or derive the edge from the guard and refTick only when spot has no active liquidity.

Fixture (PLEA is currency0 in this run, pleaIsZero == true, market band [-120720, 887220]; mirror the ticks for the other ordering): alice buys 100 IMD; skip(DEADMAN + 1); the distributor transfers 50,000,000 PLEA to alice; alice sells 50,000,000 PLEA exact-in through BuyRouter.sellExactInLimit with sqrtPriceLimitX96 = TickMath.getSqrtPriceAtTick(marketLower - 6000) = tick -126720. Expected (README): revert, no wall at a drained tick. Actual: wall() == (lower -887220, upper -126720, liquidity 6891500741742536247649), wallImd == 12.21129 IMD: the full reserve sits 6,000 ticks below the market band's lower tick and 5,999 below the guard.

### 4. Info: The pool opens at a 5,720.83 IMD market cap, not the documented 5,700: seed() aligns the opening tick to the 60-tick grid away from the target price

`src/PleaHook.sol:380`

```
            lower = _alignUp(tick);
```

README ('creates the pool at a 5,700 IMD market cap'), THREAT-MODEL invariant 20 and seed()'s natspec say 5,700 IMD (14,000 on the mainnet branch). seed() computes the exact sqrt price for MARKET_CAP_IMD over 1e9 PLEA, then aligns the single-sided band's edge to TICK_SPACING = 60 away from that price (lower = _alignUp(tick) when PLEA is token0, upper = _alignDown(tick) when PLEA is token1), both toward a higher IMD-per-PLEA price so the position is PLEA-only. The first buy therefore trades at the aligned tick, up to 60 ticks (0.6%) above the target; at the actual target tick the alignment lands 37 ticks away, so the opening cap is 5,720.83 IMD in both orderings (0.37% above 5,700; the same rounding applies to 14,000 on mainnet). Bounded by one tick spacing, always in the direction that keeps the seed PLEA-only, no constant changed: a documentation precision note, not a loss. From audit_math (info). Fix (optional): document the opening cap as 'at least 5,700 IMD, within one 60-tick spacing', or state the aligned figure.

### 5. Info: The cap's daily allowance banks over idle days without limit: one buy after a quiet stretch may ratchet the cap down by many days' worth at once, unlike the wall guard's one-day idle cap

`src/PleaHook.sol:749`

```
            uint256 budget = rate * elapsed;
```

_applyCap computes the allowance from the whole time since lastCapDecayAt (budget = rate * elapsed, and lastCapDecayAt only advances by the time whose allowance was actually consumed), so after N idle days a single buy may lower the cap by N days of capDecayPerDay in one block. The wall guard deliberately caps idle time at one day (elapsed = 1 days in _updateWallGuard), so the two paces read differently from README 'Cap pace' ('at most (seeded - 900k) / 15 PLEA a day'). Invariant 21's wording (per day, floor reachable in 15 days) holds on average and the cap never falls below the holdings or 900k, so the only effect is that the first trim after a quiet stretch burns more than a reader of the README expects. Design and documentation note. From audit_permissions (info). If one day is the intended maximum per swap, cap elapsed at 1 day as the guard does (and move lastCapDecayAt to now on every swap).

**Reproduction**

Fixture, after the launch window: buy(alice, 5_000e18) (the cap follows the buy by a fraction of a day's allowance); skip(10 days); buy(bob, 5_000e18). Measured (test/scratch/A3Judge.t.sol test_capAllowanceBanksAcrossIdleDays): the second buy lowers inventoryCap by 546,066,119.99 PLEA = exactly 10.000 days of capDecayPerDay (54,606,611.99 PLEA) in one swap; cap 270,482,225 PLEA against holdings 264,939,475 PLEA. Under a one-day-per-swap reading the drop would be at most 54.6M PLEA.
