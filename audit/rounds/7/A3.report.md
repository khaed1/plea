## Findings

### 1. Medium: A trim takes the removed PLEA from the PoolManager before the seller settles, so after the death a sell larger than the pool's PLEA inventory reverts through every swap-then-settle router

`src/PleaHook.sol:794`

```
            poolManager.take(Currency.wrap(plea), address(this), pleaOut);
```

`_applyCap` runs inside `afterSwap`. When a sell leaves the market band above `inventoryCap` it removes the excess liquidity and immediately takes the removed PLEA as ERC-20 from the PoolManager to burn it (line 794). At that point the seller's PLEA input is not yet in the PoolManager: every standard v4 router (Uniswap's V4Router and Universal Router, the fixture's BuyRouter) calls `swap` first and `sync`/`settle`s the input afterwards, so the PoolManager holds only the PLEA that was in the pool before the swap (the market band plus the wall). The IMD side guards exactly this case with `own`/`asClaims` and ERC-6909 claims (R3-A3-1, `retainClaims`); the PLEA side has no such guard. Once the cap has caught up with the holdings (the ratchet reaches it within 15 days of quiet buying), the excess over the cap equals the whole fill, so any sale larger than the pool's pre-swap PLEA makes the trim's take revert with `ERC20InsufficientBalance(poolManager, held, pleaOut)` and the whole swap fails. While the Cabal lives this is unreachable (a gated sale is at most 2.5M PLEA, far under any realistic holding). After the death, when THREAT-MODEL invariant 1 says every restriction ends forever and invariant 21 says PLEA sold back above the cap is trimmed, a holder whose position exceeds the pool's remaining inventory cannot sell it in one swap through any ordinary router: the sale must be split into chunks no larger than the pool's PLEA (each intermediate trim thins the band, so the chunks fill at a worse total price) or go through a custom router that settles its PLEA before calling `swap`. No funds are lost; the sale reverts atomically. Severity Medium under section 4: a bounded DoS of a path invariant 1 promises is free, with a workaround. The economics specialist's partial-fill finding is a separate mechanism (IMD side, `_payFees`) and is reported on its own. Fix (preserving the design): do for PLEA what `retainClaims` does for IMD: in `_applyCap` (and, for symmetry, `_closeWall`, though that one runs inside the hook's own unlock where the PoolManager already holds the PLEA) mint the removed PLEA to the hook as ERC-6909 claims (`poolManager.mint(address(this), _id(plea), pleaOut)`), keep a `retainPleaClaims` counter, and burn/take/`IPLEA.burn` it in `_rebalance`; or take `min(pleaOut, plea.balanceOf(poolManager))` now and mint the rest as claims. The hook is 218 bytes under EIP-170, so the smaller of the two variants is the one to pick. Invariants checked: 1, 19, 21.

**Reproduction**

Sepolia settings (IMD as currency0; the same happens with PLEA as currency0 since only the take order matters), a fresh PoolManager. 1) Right after the 90-minute window alice buys with 20,000 IMD exact-in through a plain swap-then-settle router; she receives about 660M PLEA and the market keeps about 156M. 2) 20 days pass; bob's 1 IMD buy ratchets `inventoryCap` down to the holdings (about 156M; the cap travels (820M - 900k) / 15 per day, so 20 days are enough). 3) The dead-man (33 hours with no verdict) has passed: `plea.restricted()` is false. 4) alice sells her whole balance exact-in, no price limit, through the same router. Expected: the sale fills, 0.25% of the fill is burned, the excess over the cap is trimmed and its IMD goes to the wall claims. Actual: `PoolManager.swap` reverts with `WrappedError(hook, afterSwap.selector, WrappedError(PLEA, transfer, ERC20InsufficientBalance(poolManager, 156,340,207e18, 658,704,060e18)))`: the trim asks for 658.7M PLEA while the PoolManager holds 156.3M, because alice's 660M input is settled only after `swap` returns. Proof: test/scratch/TrimTakesUnsettledPlea.t.sol (self-contained, no fixture) fails on this code with that revert and passes once the trim keeps the removed PLEA as ERC-6909 claims or takes no more than the PoolManager holds (its post-fix assertions only require the sale to go through, the fee to burn and the market to sit at the cap).

### 2. Low: A partially filled exact-input buy reverts when its ERC-20 IMD refund drains the PoolManager below the payouts: `_refund` runs before `_payFees`, whose claims test uses the pre-swap own IMD and never

`src/PleaHook.sol:600`

```
            bool asClaims = own < out && imd.balanceOf(address(poolManager)) < out;
```

In `afterSwap` the unearned specified-side fee is refunded first (line 523, `_refund`), and `_refund` pays it as ERC-20 IMD taken from the PoolManager whenever the PoolManager's IMD balance covers it (line 647). `_payFees` then decides whether the 1.5% payouts are paid as claims with `own < out && balanceOf(PM) < out`, where `own` is this pool's IMD before the swap (market band + wall + retainClaims). The refund has already spent part of that same IMD, but `own` does not see it: when `own >= out` the claims path is skipped and the plain `take` of the owner / prize / cashback shares runs against a PoolManager that now holds less than `out`, so the IMD transfer reverts (`ERC20InsufficientBalance`) and the whole swap fails. The failure band is non-monotonic: the same price-limited buy succeeds when slightly smaller (the refund leaves enough) and when slightly larger (the refund itself exceeds the PoolManager's IMD and is paid as claims, the documented R2-A3-5 path). Invariant 19 and THREAT-MODEL section 3 say a partial fill of an exact-input trade pays on the fill and refunds the rest (as ERC-6909 claims whenever it exceeds the PoolManager's IMD); here it reverts instead. No funds are lost (the trade reverts atomically); the trader's own price-limited buy is refused while the pool's IMD is small and the PoolManager holds little other IMD (a fresh chain, Sepolia's TestIMD; on mainnet other IMD pools in the same PoolManager shrink the band). Low: an edge case of the trader's own limit with no loss. Fix: decide the refund's form and the payouts' form from one budget: compute whether the PoolManager can pay `out + refund` before refunding (pay the refund as claims when it cannot), or pay the payouts before the refund, or in `_payFees` test `balanceOf(PM) < out` alone once the flash-take case is already excluded by `own`. Invariants checked: 19 (partial fills), section 3's refund-as-claims rule.

**Reproduction**

Default fixture (test/utils/Fixture.sol: Sepolia settings, past the launch window, the PoolManager holds only this pool's IMD). alice buys 20 IMD exact-in (PoolManager IMD = 20e18). Take as `limit` the sqrt price a 100 IMD buy reaches (snapshot, buy 100 IMD, read `currentSqrtPriceX96`, revert). carol calls `BuyRouter.buyExactInLimit(g * 1e18, abi.encode(carol), limit)` for g from 1000 to 1400 in steps of 20 (each in its own snapshot). Measured (test/scratch/RefundThenPayoutBand.t.sol): g = 1000..1160 succeed with the refund as ERC-20; g = 1180, 1200, 1220, 1240, 1260 revert with `WrappedError(hook, afterSwap, WrappedError(IMD, transfer, ERC20InsufficientBalance(poolManager, balance, needed)))` from `poolManager.take` inside `_payImd` (g = 1200: balance 0.098e18 left after the refund, 0.492e18 needed; g = 1220: balance 1 wei, 0.246e18 needed); g = 1280..1400 succeed again with the refund paid as ERC-6909 claims. Every successful size fills to the same limit (16,646,388 PLEA). Expected: every size fills to the limit and refunds the unearned fee (tokens or claims). Actual: a band of sizes reverts.
